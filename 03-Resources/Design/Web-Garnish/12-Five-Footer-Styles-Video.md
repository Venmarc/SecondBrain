## The five styles

### 1. Hero Footer
A **rounded card** floats at the bottom of the page over a light ground. The card builds up in steps: centered brand heading → subheading → contact info → quick-link columns. Everything lives inside the card; the page above stays empty.
**Video example**: Vyrex Light Coldwear — dark navy card, brand statement top-center, then four link/contact columns.
**Fit**: gives a small footer presence without height; the card edge does the section work.

### 2. Silhouette Footer
An illustration is flattened into a **solid-color silhouette mass that IS the footer**. The light page sits above; the silhouette's top edge is the transition. Logo and link columns sit inside the color mass.
**Video example**: Cyclleon — green treeline silhouette rising into a pale yellow page.
**Fit**: the illustration can be any subject; the shape doubles as the page-to-footer seam.

### 3. Large-type Footer
Link columns sit above; a **giant brand logotype** fills the remaining empty space of the band, edge to edge. The type IS the art.
**Video example**: Astralis — blue wordmark spanning the full footer width.
**Fit**: cheapest bold move — pure typography, no asset.

### 4. Negative Footer
An oversized logotype or brand element is placed **into the background as negative space** — the wordmark is cut through or behind the footer surface, content overlays it.
**Video example**: brün — green block with the white logotype bleeding off both edges.
**Fit**: brand presence that stays quiet behind the links.

### 5. Grounded Footer
A full-width **image is grounded at the very bottom edge**; the nav and content float in open space above it. The image anchors the page like ground under a horizon.
**Video example**: ALTENNIS — sunset field photo at the bottom, content in open space above.
**Fit**: emotional close; image choice carries the mood.

## Shared rules the reel implies

1. **One viewport, max.** None of the examples overflow a screen; a footer taller than one viewport reads as its own page.
2. **One bold element carries the footer.** Card, silhouette, type, negative wordmark, or image — exactly one. The rest stays quiet.
3. **The seam is designed.** Page-to-footer transition flows via shape or color; no hard unstyled cut (except Deliberate-grounded, where the image edge IS the seam).
4. **Smooth, not busy.** Examples animate build-up or hover only; the structure itself is calm.
