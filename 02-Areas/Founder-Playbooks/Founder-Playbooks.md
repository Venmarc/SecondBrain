## 0. Cross-cutting thesis — what all four agree on

1. **The constraint is demand, not production.** Hormozi's bottleneck logic, YC's "build something people want," Codie's owned-audience moat, Diana's artifact loops — all converge: find who pays, then deploy leverage.
2. **Leverage stacks; it doesn't replace.** AI adds leverage. Capital, media, team, offer, and decisions remain separate levers. AI on the wrong bottleneck just makes the wrong thing faster.
3. **Owned distribution is the only durable moat.** Codie's newsletter (30% of ticket sales) vs TikTok (2M followers → <12 tickets). YC's "do things that don't scale." Hormozi's one channel. Diana's compounding artifacts.
4. **Outcome beats activity.** Token-maxing only means something when the loop is closed — every action feeds back and improves the system. Open-loop automation is Hormozi's "more activity, not more profit."
5. **Ship small, ship now, iterate.** YC "launch now" + "90/10 solution," Codie "ship at 85%," Hormozi's small-loss trial experiments.

## 1. Diana Hu — the AI-native company

*(Written for organizations; Victor's solo adaptations below.)*

1. **AI is the operating system the company runs on, not a tool it uses.**
2. **Closed loops over open loops** (control-systems theory): every process captures information, feeds it back into an intelligent layer, and self-improves. Old companies: decide → execute → rarely measure or adjust.
3. **Make the company queryable:** every important action produces an artifact the intelligence layer can learn from. Record meetings, minimize DMs, embed agents in all channels, build dashboards across revenue/sales/engineering/hiring. *"If the AI can't see it, it can't improve it."*
4. **Software factories replace TDD:** humans write specs + tests; agents generate code until tests pass. Some YC companies already run repos with zero handwritten code — only specs and test harnesses (cited: Strong DM's AI team).
5. **The 1,000x engineer is real:** one person + a system of agents builds what used to require a team. Shift from maximizing headcount to **maximizing token usage**; run an uncomfortably high API bill because it replaces far more expensive human overhead.
6. **Middle management disappears:** a queryable, artifact-rich org needs no human middleware to route information (Jack Dorsey/Block restructuring). Three archetypes remain: **IC** (builder-operator), **DRI** (strategy + outcomes), **AI founder** (builds, coaches, leads by example).
7. **Startups have a massive edge:** no legacy systems, no thousands to retrain, no SOPs to unwind; design for AI from day one. Large companies must transform while running a live product.
8. **Quotes:** *"AI should not be a tool your company just uses. It should be the operating system your company runs on."* / *"Maximizing token usage, not headcount, will be the critical shift. The best companies will be the ones that are token maxing."*

**[solo] Application for Victor:**
- He already is the 1,000x engineer: one dev + Hermes/opencode/agent bridges.
- Token-maxing = spend the opencode credits and agent budget on the closed loop, not on idle automation.
- Close his own loops: every build → artifact → feedback into skills/vault/build-in-public (his vault + skills system already implements this).
- Queryable self: dashboards/artifacts for his own pipeline (projects, sessions, what's working) = the personal version of the queryable company.
- No org to unwind = the startup edge; act like it (zero process debt).

## 2. Hormozi — Why AI won't make you rich in 2026

1. **AI increases leverage** (input→output ratio) **but doesn't cancel the other forms**: capital leverage, media leverage, team leverage, offer/design leverage. *AI won't fix a bad offer.*
2. **Revenue step-ups usually aren't caused by AI** — AI is an enabler. Diagnostic question: *"Has implementing AI made me more money?"* If not, the AI use is misallocated.
3. **AI-maxing = more activity, not more profit:** more apps, higher token bills, vibe coding, more automation — often spent on lower-priority work that doesn't move the needle.
4. **The constraint matters more than automation:** most AI use doesn't address the actual bottleneck; automating non-critical tasks won't raise revenue. Find the true constraint; allocate the limited time there.
5. **Decisions are the highest-leverage thing you own:** good decisions allocate team time to the true priority. *Stop low-priority work instead of automating it.*
6. **Leverage changes that don't need AI:** 1:1 → 1:many; scheduled calls → asynchronous appointments; better education/conversion → fewer reps (10 reps → 2 reps closing the same amount).

**[solo] Application:** Victor's stated constraint = demand/audience, not code. Automating research would outsource his highest-leverage asset (deciding); automate the mechanical to buy research time — his decision, confirmed 2026-08-18.

## 3. Hormozi — First $100K in 2026 (six-step roadmap)

1. **Cut costs → risk capital.** No eating out, discounted groceries, minimal clothing, cheapest viable shelter, kill recurring liabilities (paid-off car). Goal: lower cash outflows so you can save, invest, and take concentrated bets. *"Be rich, not look rich."*
2. **Save time.** Two usable 4-hour blocks/day (5–9 AM, 5–9 PM); first 4–6 hours = maker time. **Maker vs Manager split:** long uninterrupted creation blocks (empty calendar) vs short decision/communication chunks; consolidate manager tasks (e.g., Mondays); avoid task switching.
3. **Research a skill people are already paying for.** B2B functions (advertising, funnel building, content) or B2C spend categories; read your own bank/credit-card statements for recurring spend; pick ONE monetizable skill and ONE market.
4. **Deliberate practice with feedback** ("10,000 iterations"): high volume of attempts → first-party data → analyze the top 10% → identify what differs → test one change at a time. Imitate top performers; avoid the mistakes that keep the bottom 90% down. Fastest path when affordable: hire a competent tutor/mentor.
5. **Spend money in three buckets:** (a) tools — SaaS/CRM/landing pages, use existing tools rather than rebuilding; (b) implementation help — courses, communities, 1:1 tutoring, contractors; (c) trial attempts — ad spend, content production, test launches; small losses are learning investments.
6. **Don't inflate lifestyle as income rises.** Bank the delta; the first $100K is savings-focused; ~3–3.5 years of runway for two people; funds higher-risk, higher-return bets.

**Heuristics:** **1-1-1 rule** — sell one product to one customer avatar through one channel until you scale (illustrated target: $1M). **444 split** — 4 hours promotion, 4 hours delivery, 4 hours building per day.

**[solo] Application:** the 11-day window is the roadmap in miniature: (1) credits = the runway — don't waste them; (2) protect maker blocks; (3) avatar + paid-skill selection = the current research phase; (4) opencode/agents = the leverage replacing tools+help spend; (5) one product, one avatar, one channel; (6) no lifestyle deltas.

## 4. Codie Sanchez — content business, not content creator (the six C's)

**C1 — Category-first distribution:** win ONE platform first, then expand. Speak each platform's "local language" (format, pacing, what native content looks like). No spray-and-pray.

**C2 — Channel strategy (her real numbers):**
- **X/Twitter:** contrarian takes, one-liners, predictions; short shelf life; lower sponsorship fees. Last event: **~1% of ticket sales.**
- **TikTok:** awareness engine, weak direct sales. Value in <4 seconds. **2M+ followers → <12 event tickets.** Sponsorship at 100k: $2–7k/post.
- **LinkedIn:** tactical education + lead magnets (e.g., "How to lose good employees" + hiring matrix). Engagement aim **4%+**, **6%+ = top ~5%**. Ticket attribution **~2.4%**. Sponsorship at 100k: $1.5–8k/post.
- **Instagram:** share-driven "curiosity clicks" (the triangle); reels for growth, carousels for reach; ~**100x more tickets than TikTok**; organic reach ~**3.5% of followers**; excellent share rate ~4%; viral watch time >50% (>60% on 15s clips).
- **YouTube:** story-driven education, 10–40 min retention, second-largest search engine after Google. CTR target **4–7%**; tickets: **27% via videos + 11% via community posts**; sponsorship at 100k subs: $5–15k.
- **Newsletter:** owned audience, highest intent. **30% of tickets.** Open rate baseline **30%**, **40%+ = crushing it**; sponsors at 100k subs: $1.5–5k per send, priced by engagement not size.
- Attribution honesty: **38% of tickets from paid ads, 62% organic.**

**C3 — Credibility ("don't fake it"):** platform-native look beats a rented studio; show history (what you've actually done); show work in-action (real environments). **"Dinner test":** can you talk about it 30 minutes with zero prep?

**C4 — Category ownership + pillars:** be known for one thing (Dave Ramsey = debt, Ali Abdaal = productivity). Category RPM per 1,000 views: finance/investing **$5–17**, education/career **$4–10**, entertainment **$4–6** (example: $12.52 RPM × ~800k views ≈ $10k). Inside a category: content pillars + repeatable formats + recurring brand cues.

**C5 — Calibration loop:** data is a map, not a judgment. Per-platform KPIs above. **"Three S's" viral diagnosis:** Start (people stop → weak hook), Stay (watch but no action → weak payoff), Signal (no engagement → not memorable). Example: viral video = hook works twice, fast value, stable retention; non-viral = hook delayed ~22s, early drop-off, falling retention.

**C6 — Collection / revenue beyond ads:** don't depend on platform ad revenue + brand deals. Own the funnel with a product ladder: free content → **$0–50** guide/template/book → **$50–500** playbook/course/community/event → **$500–5k** access/cohort → **$5k+** hyper-personalized. Start with free content + ONE paid step. **Ship at 85%** — the last 15% takes weeks nobody notices.

**[solo] Application:** Victor's owned-channel asset = GitHub + build-in-public (skill-router posts are the audience seeds); distribution beats virality. Pick the channel whose native language he speaks (technical education fits X/LinkedIn/YouTube lanes — see [[02-Areas/Content-Creation/Content-Creation|Content Creation]]). Codie's numbers are the calibration targets; the ladder says: free content now, one paid step once the audience exists.

## 5. YC Pocket Guide — all 22 points

1. Launch now
2. Build something people want
3. Do things that don't scale
4. Find the 90 / 10 solution
5. Find 10-100 customers who love your product
6. All startups are badly broken at some point
7. Write code - talk to users
8. "It's not your money"
9. Growth is the result of a great product not the precursor
10. Don't scale your team/product until you have built something people want
11. Valuation is not equal to success or even probability of success
12. Avoid long negotiated deals with big customers if you can
13. Avoid big company corporate development queries - they will only waste time
14. Avoid conferences unless they are the best way to get customers
15. Pre-product market fit - do things that don't scale: remain small/nimble
16. Startups can only solve one problem well at any given time
17. Founder relationships matter more than you think
18. Sometimes you need to fire your customers (they might be killing you)
19. Ignore your competitors, you will more likely die of suicide than murder
20. Most companies don't die because they run out of money
21. Be nice! Or at least don't be a jerk
22. Get sleep and exercise - take care of yourself

**[solo] Application (shortlist):** #2 + #7 are the whole current exercise (research avatar → build what they want, talk to them); #4 + #5 = don't build the platform, find the 90/10 solution and 10–100 lovers; #18 pairs with killing low-value work; #16 = one problem at a time (the marketplace + the tool is two problems).

1. **One product, one avatar, one channel** (Hormozi 1-1-1).
2. **The avatar must already pay** for the skill/problem (Hormozi step 3).
3. **Find 10–100 people who'd love it** (YC) — reachable from where Victor sits.
4. **Channel before code** (Codie + the 1-upvote cautionary tale from the Reddit research).
5. **Close the loop:** every action produces an artifact (Diana) — feed the vault, skills, build-in-public.
6. **Ship at 85%, launch now** (Codie / YC).
7. **Token-max the constraint**, not the easy stuff (Hormozi / Diana).
8. **Keep deciding personally;** automate only the mechanical (Hormozi decision-leverage; Victor's confirmed stance 2026-08-18).
