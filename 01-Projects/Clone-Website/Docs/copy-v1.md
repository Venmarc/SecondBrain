## 0. Voice Spec (the rules copy obeys)

1. A person who happens to run a lab. Warm, precise, unhurried.
2. Short declaratives. Average 12 words. Nothing over 25.
3. No em dashes. Ever.
4. Never hype. Banned: revolutionary, disrupt, unlock, seamless, journey, miracle, game-changing, one-of-a-kind (in body copy).
5. Address the reader as "you." Never "clients," "customers," "users."
6. A clinical term appears once, then gets translated. "Somatic cell nuclear transfer" shows up a single time. After that: "we place your DNA inside an emptied egg."
7. Specific numbers over adjectives. 14, not "several." 400, not "hundreds."
8. The product is emotional. The voice is steady. The reader can cry. The copy can't.
9. Never promise sameness or forever. Banned claims: "the same person," "identical in every way," "immortality," "eternal." Allowed: "the same genome," "a continuation," "a second beginning."
10. No questions in headlines except the FAQ.
11. Zero exclamation marks on the entire site.
12. The fiction holds everywhere on the site. The reveal email is the only honest page, and it is honest about being the only one.
13. We never claim a collective benefit. No overpopulation talk, no "for mankind," no saving the world. Private product, private justification.
14. We quote our critics. Confidence is the register.

## 4. Section 2 — "From One, Another" (the process)

Structure mirrors White Desert's 6-step "How it works." Each step: title, 2–3 sentences, one term introduced and translated.

**Step 1. The Conversation**
You meet a Codon Labs physician, by video. We talk through your health history, your reasons, and what a continuation is and is not. This part is free, and it is the part that matters most.

**Step 2. The Sample**
A dermatologist takes a small skin biopsy, four millimeters, under local anesthetic. Your cells go into culture and into cryogenic storage. From this day, your option never expires. (Visual: hands in gloves, the vial, macro.)

**Step 3. The Reading**
We sequence your entire genome, twice, on independent machines, and reconcile the results. You receive the complete report. It is yours. It leaves with you. (This is the genome-cost-curve beat: what took a billion dollars in 2003 takes us a day.)

**Step 4. The Reprogramming**
This is the part the world waited 27 years for. We take a donated egg cell, remove its nucleus, and place one of your cells inside it. An electric pulse and a chemical signal convince the cell it is young again. It forgets being skin. It remembers how to become everything. (Visual: the microscopic footage, abstract, warm-toned. This is the hero loop's little sibling.)

**Step 5. The Culture**
Five days in the lab. The cell divides, becomes a blastocyst, and passes quality gates at every stage. We do not rush this. Nothing proceeds on a schedule. Everything proceeds on evidence.

**Step 6. The Beginning**
One embryo, transferred, with the consent and full medical care of a surrogate carried by our program. Nine months later, a person. Yours, in the only way that matters: legally yours, entirely herself. (Visual: the infant hand gripping the donor finger. The single most important image on the site.)

### FAQ — Register A: myth debunking (gold)

**Will my continuation age faster than me?**
No. The fear comes from Dolly the sheep, the first cloned mammal, born in Scotland in 1996. She developed arthritis young and died at six, and the myth was born with her. But in 2016, University of Nottingham scientists published a long-term study of thirteen cloned sheep, four of them cloned from the very same cells as Dolly. Nine years old, normal blood pressure, normal metabolism, normal joints. They aged normally. Dolly's arthritis is now thought to be the concrete floors and photo-call treats, not her origin.

**Does my continuation get my memories?**
No. Memories are not in DNA. Nothing in your head is copied. Everything she will ever know, she learns, the way you did.

**Will the baby come out grown, like in the movies?**
No. Movies skip 18 years because 18 years is a hard watch. A continuation is born a baby and grows on the world's clock. There is no fast version of a person.

**Are clones less healthy than other people?**
The early animal data looked rough because the technique was young. The 2023 reprogramming breakthrough is the difference. Our first 14 continuations are alive and developing normally, under the largest pediatric genetics follow-up program ever run. We publish the numbers.

### FAQ — Register B: plausible questions (good)

**Who is the legal parent?**
You are. Parentage is established before transfer, by pre-birth order under the law where the Program operates. Your continuation is legally your child before she is born. That is not a side effect of the program. It is the program.

**Can I clone someone else? My child? My late husband? My late grandfather?**
Only with their own yes, given while living, in writing, after the Conversation. If your grandfather banked his cells and signed his consent before he died, his continuation is his choice being carried out, and we will carry it out. But no one is ever continued without their own consent. Death does not withdraw a yes. Silence does. We do not work from the unwilling, the unaware, or the never-asked. This is not a legal line for us. It is the whole point. A continuation has to be chosen by the person continuing.

**What does the $2,000,000 include?**
Everything from first Conversation to first birthday. Sequencing, culture, reprogramming, surrogate matching and full prenatal care, delivery with a neonatal team standing by, and pediatric genetics support through year one. Payment is staged by milestone. If the program stops at a gate, the remaining balance stays yours.

**How long does it take?**
Fourteen months from sample to birth, today. Our 2028 goal is nine. We are not getting cheaper. We are getting faster. **[roadmap: SPEED, per stamp]**

**Where does this happen?**
The research is in Basel, where the company began in 1973. The animal research is in Allschwil and Saint-Louis, across the border. The Continuation Program operates in New York State, in the United States, where no federal statute prohibits human reproductive cloning and the state does not prohibit it. We do not accept residents of the countries or the states that have banned it.

**Do you clone pets?**
No. Pets are how this science got funded, and we are fond of them. Humans are the hard problem, and the one we chose.

**What if it doesn't take?**
The program re-attempts at no additional cost until a live birth. Your cells remain in culture for as long as you remain in the program. Nobody has exited waiting.

**Why not just have a baby?**
You might. A child is the best thing most people ever make. But a child is a stranger you get to meet. A continuation is a particular stranger: yours, doubled. People apply when the second one is the answer to something the first one cannot be. We don't explain it better than that.

**Is it legal?**
Human reproductive cloning is prohibited in about forty-six countries, including Switzerland and France, which is why the science and the Program live in different places. In the United States there is no federal prohibition, and New York does not prohibit it. Parentage runs through New York's Child-Parent Security Act, which licenses surrogacy organizations and issues pre-birth parentage orders. We decline residents of every jurisdiction that has banned cloning. The full legal review is public.
