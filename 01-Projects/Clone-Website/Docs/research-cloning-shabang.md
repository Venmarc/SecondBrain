> **One-line Summary**: Tavily deep-dive into what human cloning actually is, means, costs, and how it's sold — the source of truth for the site's copy, imagery, story, and pricing. 21 searches + 3 extracts, 2026-09-13. Structured synthesis; raw search piles in session log.

## The one-paragraph answer Victor asked for

The cloning shabang is **not** a copy of you. It is a delayed identical twin: a new person, gestated from your DNA, who shares your genome but none of your memories, starts life as a baby on a normal clock, and grows up to be genetically you but developmentally someone else [1][2][3]. People who buy clones know this — the dog cloners say it out loud: "They're 100% physically identical. But I was never in a delusion that they would be the same dog" [4]. And still they pay, because the thing being bought was never "the same dog" — it's continuity, the refusal of an ending [4][5]. That gap between what the buyer imagines and what the science delivers is the entire emotional territory of this website. The honest company sells the twin; the customer buys the second chance; the site's job is to hold both without lying.

## 1. The demand side: why people actually pay

- Grief is the entry point. Pet-cloning demand centers on anticipatory grief — relationships with animals described as "the most reliable and important" in people's lives, sometimes more painful to lose than human family [5].
- The self-clone demand is real and measurable: Theos/YouGov 2022 survey, 5,153 UK adults — 11% of men and 4% of women agreed "I would like to clone myself if I could." Religious belief had no effect; confidence in science knowledge did (14% among the highly confident). Framed by researchers as "scientific immortality" [6][7].
- What people imagine they're buying: immortality, legacy, a do-over. What the Quora/transhumanist crowd says when corrected: "your clone will live her own life" [8]. The fiction's customers are in the first group; the company's honesty has to live in the second.
- ViaGen has a 5–7 month waitlist at $50k/dog — scarcity is part of the real register [9].

## 2. What a clone actually is (the science register for section 3)

- SCNT: nucleus of an adult body cell into an enucleated egg → embryo. Dolly 1996. Primates 2018 (Zhong Zhong & Hua Hua, macaques) [10].
- A donor-clone pair could be **more** physically alike than identical twins in some traits, because they don't share a twin pregnancy's adversities (two-thirds of MZ twins share circulation in utero, causing size/health differences) [1].
- Similarity is trait-dependent: strongest for height, brain waves, intelligence; moderate for personality, vocation, orientation; "the only correct response is that a cloned child may be very similar to a donor parent in all traits, some traits, or a few traits" [1].
- Telomere/aging fears were largely a myth: Dolly's arthritis was likely environment (concrete floors, treats), and her 13 cloned sisters aged normally with no early disease (Sinclair 2016, Nature Communications) [11][12][13]. Good for the fiction: "clones age faster" is a debunked myth the site can gracefully address in FAQ.
- The real unsolved wall is epigenetic reprogramming — most researchers believe current SCNT cannot produce a healthy human carried to term [10][14]. **This is our fiction's fictional breakthrough**: "we solved reprogramming." Everything else in the science is real.

## 3. The twin experience — what "meeting a second you" feels like

- Identity under erasure: "it feels like I'm not my own person a lot of the time because people see no differences between us" (identical twin, 16) [15].
- Shared memory confusion: twins report "mixed memories from things in the past that both of us believe that we alone experienced" [15].
- Twin grief is its own category: a 70-year-old widow of her twin: "I have lost other loved ones, including my daughter, but I can't describe for others what it was like to lose my twin sister" [16].
- The clone's situation is arguably harder: an identical twin arrives with you; a clone arrives into your already-lived life, seeing their own possible futures already played out. Parental-expectation worry: would donor parents impose unfair expectations on genetic replicas? [1]
- Fiction-ready detail: Hailsham in Never Let Me Go existed to prove clones had souls — by collecting their art [17]. The strongest single beat available for the "proof of humanity" section.

## 4. History's fake announcements — the credibility recipe (Clonaid as anti-pattern)

- Clonaid (Raelian, Bahamas-registered) announced "Baby Eve" Dec 27, 2002 at a Hollywood FL Holiday Inn press conference; promised DNA verification that never came; scientific establishment ridiculed it; a year later NPR: "no proof the baby exists" [18][19][20].
- Zavos & Antinori 2001: announced intent, 1,300 US volunteers, ship in international waters / unnamed Mediterranean country; condemned universally; never delivered [21][22].
- The anti-pattern list (what our fiction must NOT do): cult ties, hotels, unverifiable press conferences, "weeks away" timelines, no peer review.
- The positive pattern (what real frontier biotech does): Colossal — "world's first de-extinction company," named credentialed scientists (Beth Shapiro), real published work, audacious-but-dated goals (mammoth calves by 2028), $200M+ raised, critics calling it overpromise [23][24][25]. Our fiction is Colossal-shaped: first-mover framing, named science, published evidence, dated milestones, visible critics.

## 5. Law: the real gaps the fiction can stand on

- ~46 countries have banned human reproductive cloning — "less than a quarter of all countries." In most of the world it is simply not legislated [26].
- US: no federal ban; FDA asserts authority over the experiments; state patchwork — most states "not legislated" (TX, NY, WA, etc.) [27][28].
- The UN declaration (2005) is non-binding; the Costa Rica total-ban treaty effort failed [29][30].
- Fiction device: a "Where we operate" / compliance page citing the real patchwork. Believable because it's true. The ship-in-international-waters idea (Zavos) reads cartoonish — avoid [21].

## 6. Money: the real price ladder under our $5M/$10M

- Dog/cat clone $50k, horse $85k (ViaGen) [9]. First commercial pet clone (Little Nicky, 2004): $50k [31].
- Elite surrogacy (the "Kim Kardashian style"): $250k–$300k+ all-in [32]. Luxury IVF cycle: $100k+; medical-tourism fertility flows run $100–130k [33].
- Human genome sequencing: $95M (2001) → ~$500 (2022), 190,000-fold drop, faster than Moore's Law [34][35]. The intro-section backbone for "procreation → replication": the cost of reading life collapsed; the fiction claims the cost of copying it is next.
- Our $5M sits ~20x above the most expensive elite surrogacy — exactly the "first-of-its-kind" premium tier. $10M for "adult" needs a definitional fork (see Open Decisions).

## 7. The fiction register (how fictional clone companies present)

- Tyrell Corp: "More human than human." [36] Gattaca: genetic legitimacy as corporate culture. These are the villain registers — our fiction should quote the genre's warnings rather than cosplay them.
- The Prestige / Moon: the adult-copy trap — both stories are about what goes wrong when you expect a copy of an adult. The honest-fiction route (clone = baby = delayed twin) steers us away from that trap entirely [37].
- Wilmut's own regret: "Human cloning has grabbed people's imagination, but that is merely a diversion and one we personally regret and find distasteful" [38]. The fiction can quote its critics — luxury brands do this; it signals confidence.
- Rick & Morty "Mortyplicity": decoy clones hunting decoy clones [39]. Use case: the verification story — "one, real, verifiable" vs. the decoy farce. A "how to verify" / authenticity-guarantee page is the R&M homage translated into brand copy.

## Open decisions surfaced by this research

1. **Pricing fork**: "$5M kid / $10M adult" has two readings. (a) Output age: a child clone vs. an adult clone — implies accelerated growth, The Prestige/Moon territory, a much deeper fiction. (b) Donor age at sampling: clone made from a minor donor vs. an adult donor — output is always a baby either way, matches real SCNT (adult cells reprogrammed successfully, ages 35 and 75) [40], keeps the honest-fiction line. Reading (b) is the believable one; (a) changes the entire story. Victor decides.
2. **Name**: "Codon" — no biotech company uses it; only generic tool features (e.g., Twist's codon optimizer) [41]. "Chrom" — near-collisions: Chroma Medicine and nChroma Bio are real, funded epigenetic-editing companies [42]. Codon is the cleaner mark.
3. **Hero metaphor** (proposed): Antarctica = the interior of a cell. White Desert's vast white void becomes the microscopic world rendered vast — snow-fade intro resolves into cell division. "The last unexplored continent is the one inside us." This is the White Desert skeleton translated by the voice note 3 evolution arc.
